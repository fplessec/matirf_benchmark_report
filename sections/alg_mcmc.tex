\section{MCMC --- the MMSE estimator}
\label{sec:mcmc}

\paragraph{How it works.}
MCMC is the only \emph{MMSE} method: it returns the posterior mean, not a mode, by a
Metropolis-Hastings chain averaged after a burn-in:
\begin{quote}
$f \leftarrow f_0$ \hfill\textit{(start: the ridge $s_2^2$ estimate)}\\
\textbf{repeat} for each sample:\\
\hspace{1.2em}$z \leftarrow f + (H^{\!\top}\!H+\lambda_{rr} I)^{-1} H^{\!\top}(g-Hf)$ \hfill\textit{(pull toward the data)}\\
\hspace{1.2em}$z \leftarrow z + \sigma\,\varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ \hfill\textit{(explore)}\\
\hspace{1.2em}$z \leftarrow D_\sigma(\max(z,0))$ \hfill\textit{(positivity, then denoise)}\\
\hspace{1.2em}accept $z$ with probability $\min(1,\,e^{-\Delta D/\beta})$ \hfill\textit{(Metropolis test on $D$)}\\
\textbf{return} the mean of the states after burn-in \hfill\textit{(the MMSE estimate)}
\end{quote}
\noindent where $\lambda_{rr}$ is the data-step ridge weight, $\sigma$ the exploration/denoising
level (a fraction of the peak), $\varepsilon$ Gaussian noise, $D_\sigma$ the denoiser, $\beta$ the
acceptance temperature, and $\Delta D$ the change in the data term. It is dual-free, like PnP.

\paragraph{Key parameters.}
The denoiser and $\sigma$; but the decisive knob is $\lambda_{rr}$, whose useful window is
$[s_3^2,s_2^2]\approx[0.3,36]$. $\lambda_{rr}=s_1=38.9$ sits \emph{above} it,
so the data step barely pulls and the chain drifts; bring $\lambda_{rr}\approx5$ into the window
and take enough samples --- this is the single largest lever.

\paragraph{Results.}
With $\lambda_{rr}\approx5$, MCMC \textbf{beats the ridge start on MA-TIRF} and \emph{excels} on the
well-posed deconvolution. The best denoiser is object-dependent (Table~\ref{tab:denoisers}):
Gaussian at low noise, shifting to TV~Bregman (membrane) or DCT (sparse) as noise rises.

\paragraph{Limit.}
With a good $\lambda_{rr}$ MCMC is competitive
with the MAP methods, though usually behind the plug-and-play family. Its honest limits: the
denoiser must be 3D-appropriate, and \texttt{None} (no prior) cannot beat its own start.
