\section{The inverse problem}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Forward model}
Both problems are linear with a non-negativity constraint:
\begin{equation}
g = H f + n, \qquad f \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $f$ is the unknown object, $H$ the known forward operator, $n$ the detector noise, and $g$
the measurement (peak-normalised, $\max g = 1$). For MA-TIRF, $H$ acts \emph{independently in
each lateral pixel}: it mixes the $n_z$ depth planes of a column into the $n_\theta$ measured
angle values, so it is one small $n_\theta \times n_z$ matrix applied to every column. For
deconvolution, $H$ is a convolution with a known PSF.

\subsection{Why MA-TIRF is hard: the spectrum of $H$}
The per-column matrix has singular values that collapse by orders of magnitude (synthetic
microscope: 13 angles, $n_z = 50$ over $0$--$300$~nm, physical operator, $\texttt{normalize}=$
\texttt{False}):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & $s_3$ & $s_4$ & $s_5\ldots$ \\
\midrule
$\sigma$   & $38.9$ & $6.02$ & $0.562$ & $0.033$ & $<10^{-3}$ \\
$\sigma^2$ & $1.5\times10^{3}$ & $36$ & $0.32$ & $1.1\times10^{-3}$ & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Only two to three depth directions per column are determined by the data; the other ${\sim}47$
of $50$ lie in the numerical null space of $H$. \textbf{The prior --- not the optimiser ---
decides those ${\sim}47$ directions.} This single fact drives every result in this report: the
ceiling on a reconstruction is set by the prior, and the universal failure mode is
\emph{depth collapse} (the object slides or flattens in $z$, exactly where the data are blind).

\subsection{Scale of the unknown}
Along the best-determined direction $g_1 = s_1 f_1$ with $\max g = 1$, so $f$ peaks at
$\approx 1/s_1 \approx 0.02$ on MA-TIRF (and $\approx 1$ on deconvolution). Any parameter in
units of $f$ --- a step size, a proposal noise, a sparsity threshold --- must be compared to
${\sim}0.02$, not to $1$; a default tuned for a photograph is $\approx s_1 \approx 40\times$ off.
This is the most common way an algorithm that works elsewhere silently fails here.

\subsection{Estimators and the objective}
Two estimators appear. The \emph{MAP} solvers (Adam, PPXA, ADMM, PnP, ADMM-PnP) minimise an
energy
\begin{equation}
L(f) = (1-\lambda)\, D(Hf, g) \;+\; \lambda\,\kappa\, R(f), \qquad f \ge 0,\ \lambda \in [0,1],
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
with $D$ the per-pixel negative log-likelihood --- $\|Hf-g\|^2/(2 b\, n_g)$ for Gaussian noise
of variance $b$ --- which carries the noise level and sits at $\approx \tfrac12$ at the truth
(a $\chi^2$ anchor). $R$ is the regulariser; the scalar $\kappa = 1/R(f_{\text{init}})$ rescales
it onto $D$'s scale so that $\lambda$ reads as a regularisation \emph{share} rather than a dead
knob. The \emph{MMSE} solver (MCMC) returns the posterior mean instead, by sampling. ADMM, PnP
and ADMM-PnP hard-wire their prior (a soft threshold, or a denoiser) and do not use
$R$, $\lambda$ or $\kappa$.
