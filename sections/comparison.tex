\paragraph{The denoiser depends on the object, the noise, and the algorithm --- there is no
universal winner.} This is the clearest lesson of the sweeps (Secs.~\ref{sec:pnp}--\ref{sec:mcmc}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{By object and noise.} At \emph{zero} noise a smooth Gaussian denoiser tends to win;
  \emph{under} noise the winner shifts by structure --- TV~Bregman on the continuous membrane, DCT
  on the filament network and point-like structures, Gaussian on filaments in PnP. The per-structure
  tables in each chapter show it: the same method wants a different denoiser on a different object.
\item \textbf{One firm algorithm rule.} \textbf{TV~Bregman collapses inside ADMM-PnP} (the dual
  iteration blows up its data fit) on every structure, while it is competitive-to-best in the
  dual-free PnP and MCMC. So the presence of the dual variable, more than a single ``best denoiser'',
  is what flips the ranking.
\item \textbf{DCT rehabilitated.} DCT, long thought unusable on MA-TIRF, is viable --- often second,
  sometimes first --- once fed at the right scale ($\div\text{peak}$). Bilateral and Wiener,
  consistently worst, were dropped.
\item \textbf{Sparsity owns zero noise.} On clean data ADMM's soft threshold wins on all three
  structures (it localises exactly when noise does not spread the energy); the plug-and-play family
  and MCMC take over as noise rises.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Corrections to the a priori notes.}
The benchmark overturns two claims in \texttt{docs/algorithms/}: (i) MCMC \textbf{does} beat the
ridge start on MA-TIRF (its old default $\lambda_{rr}=s_1$ under-pulled); (ii) DCT is not
categorically broken here (a scale artefact). Both are reflected in the chapters above.

\paragraph{The pattern.}
No single algorithm wins everywhere --- the report's thesis is a map, not a champion: match the
prior to the structure and the noise, and match the denoiser to \emph{both} the object and the
algorithm's mechanism (dual or not).
