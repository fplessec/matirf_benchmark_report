\section{The shared ground}
\label{sec:preamble}

Six facts the algorithm chapters rely on.

\paragraph{Forward model.}
Both problems are linear with positivity:
\begin{equation}
  g = Hf + n, \qquad f \ge 0, \qquad \max g = 1,
  \label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
$f$ the object, $H$ the operator (for MA-TIRF, one $n_\theta\times n_z$ matrix per lateral column,
mixing the $n_z$ depth planes into the angle values; for deconvolution, a PSF convolution), $n$ the
noise, $g$ peak-normalised.

\paragraph{Ill-posedness.}
The MA-TIRF per-column operator's singular values collapse:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & $s_3$ & $s_4$ & $s_5\dots$ \\
\midrule
$s$   & 38.9 & 6.02 & 0.562 & 0.033 & $<10^{-3}$ \\
$s^2$ & $1.5\times10^{3}$ & 36 & 0.32 & $1.1\times10^{-3}$ & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
so only $s_1,s_2$ (and partly $s_3$) are determined --- ${\sim}47$ of $50$ directions lie in
$\ker H$. The \emph{prior}, not the optimiser, fixes them: MA-TIRF is \textbf{severely ill-posed},
with \emph{depth collapse} (drift/flattening in $z$) as the failure mode. Deconvolution's spectrum
is continuous and smoothly decaying --- far better conditioned --- so ranges tied to the discrete
$s_i^2$ do not transfer.

\paragraph{Scale-invariant metrics.}
Priors recover the object's \emph{amplitude} differently, so its absolute intensity is weakly
determined while the axial \emph{structure} is the goal. The depth metrics are therefore
scale-invariant --- an $\arg\max_z$ mislocalisation and a resolved/not count, unchanged under
$f \mapsto cf$ ($c>0$). Likewise a parameter in units of $f$ is read against $f$'s own scale, which
is why the denoiser is fed $255\,f/\text{peak}$, not $255\,f$.

\paragraph{Objective.}
\textbf{Adam and PPXA} minimise
\begin{equation}
  L(f) = (1-\lambda)\, D(Hf,g) + \lambda\,\kappa\, R(f), \qquad f \ge 0,\ \lambda \in [0,1],
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
with $D = \lVert Hf-g\rVert^2/(2 b n_g)$ (Gaussian noise variance $b$, $n_g$ the pixel count of
$g$) --- a mean equal to $\tfrac12$ at the truth, so $\texttt{chi2\_ratio}=2D$ --- and
$\kappa = 1/R(f_{\text{init}})$ making $\lambda$ a regularisation \emph{share}. The others do not
minimise $L$: \textbf{ADMM} minimises its own $\ell_1$ energy; \textbf{PnP-HQS} and
\textbf{ADMM-PnP} replace the prior by a denoiser (a fixed point, no explicit energy);
\textbf{MCMC} returns the posterior \emph{mean} by sampling.

\paragraph{Ridge start.}
Every solver starts from $f_0 = (H^{\!\top}\!H + \lambda_{rr} I)^{-1} H^{\!\top} g$ with
$\lambda_{rr} = s_2^2$. A ridge weight keeps the directions $s_i^2 \gg \lambda_{rr}$ and damps
$s_i^2 \ll \lambda_{rr}$, so $s_2^2$ retains the determined part at the right scale without
amplifying noise --- far better than the adjoint $H^{\!\top} g$ (orders of magnitude too large) or
a zero start.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Each isolates a different aspect of the reconstruction $f$ against the truth $f^*$:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{NMSE} $=\lVert f-f^*\rVert^2/\lVert f^*\rVert^2$ --- overall error, amplitude
  included ($0$ perfect, ${\sim}1$ = no better than a blank image); the headline number.
\item \textbf{PSNR} $=10\log_{10}(1/\mathrm{MSE})$ [dB] --- monotone in NMSE (identical ranking),
  in familiar, range-compressed units.
\item \textbf{Depth error} [nm] $=$ mean of $\lvert\arg\max_z f - \arg\max_z f^*\rvert\cdot dz$
  over the signal columns --- the \emph{axial} mislocalisation, \emph{the} MA-TIRF question.
  Scale-invariant; ${<}\SI{6}{\nano\meter}$ (one plane) is essentially exact,
  \SIrange{50}{100}{\nano\meter} signals depth collapse.
\item \textbf{Stack recovery} $\in[0,1]$ --- the fraction of axial peak pairs at least
  \SI{30}{\nano\meter} apart that are resolved as two: axial super-resolution, i.e. whether close
  planes are separated rather than merged.
\item \textbf{SSIM} $\in[0,1]$ (deconvolution only) --- perceptual structural similarity,
  unreliable on the anisotropic 3D volume.
\item \textbf{chi2\_ratio} $=2D=$ mean residual$^2$/noise variance --- ${\approx}1$ fits to the
  noise level, ${\gg}1$ underfits, ${\ll}1$ overfits. The only metric needing \emph{no truth},
  hence the sole quality number on the real \texttt{esoubies} data.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Benchmark.}
Every number comes from one campaign on three structures --- \texttt{vesicles} (point-like),
\texttt{fibres} (filaments), \texttt{cell} (membrane) --- at noise $\{0,0.02,0.05\}$, plus 2D
\texttt{img\_001} and real \texttt{esoubies}; each run also records runtime, memory and status (an
out-of-memory on a real stack disqualifies a solver). Each solver is run at a default and a
best-found configuration (a denoiser sweep for the plug-and-play methods): the best prior and
denoiser depend on the object.
