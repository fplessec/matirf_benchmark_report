\section{PnP-HQS --- a denoiser as the prior, on a schedule}
\label{sec:pnp}

\paragraph{How it works.}
Plug-and-Play half-quadratic splitting replaces the prior's proximal operator by a
\emph{denoiser} $D_\sigma$, run over a short coarse-to-fine annealing schedule (not to
convergence):
\begin{quote}\small
$f \leftarrow (H^{\!\top}\!H+\alpha_k I)^{-1}(H^{\!\top} g + \alpha_k z)$ (data step);\quad
$z \leftarrow D_{\sigma_k}(255\,f/\text{peak})$ (denoise, scale-free);\quad $\sigma_k \downarrow$,
$\alpha_k \uparrow$ over \texttt{iter} steps.
\end{quote}
Direction by direction, $s_i^2 \gg \alpha$ follows the data and $s_i^2 \ll \alpha$ keeps the
denoised $z$: on MA-TIRF ${\sim}47$ of 50 depth directions are of the second kind, so
\textbf{the reconstruction's depth structure is essentially the denoiser's}. It has no dual
variable, so the schedule alone must drive the prior in.

\paragraph{Parameters, and what $\lambda_{kz}$ means.}
The denoiser and its level $\sigma$, and the final data weight $\lambda_{kz}=\alpha_{\text{final}}$.
By the data step, $\lambda_{kz}$ is a \textbf{threshold on the eigenvalues} $s_i^2$: it decides how
many directions follow the data at the end. On the MA-TIRF spectrum
($s_1^2{=}1500,\,s_2^2{=}36,\,s_3^2{=}0.32$), $\lambda_{kz}\approx12$ sits in the band
$(s_3^2,\,s_2^2)$, so \emph{only $s_1,s_2$ follow the data and the whole null space is handed to
the prior} --- which is what a severely ill-posed problem needs. (The DPIR default
$\lambda_{kz}=0.23$, from Kai Zhang et al., is tuned for the continuous spectrum of deconvolution,
where many directions are trusted; it under-uses the prior here.) The schedule length \texttt{iter}
is also a real lever --- more steps keep improving on this ill-posed problem, past the usual
$[8,30]$.

\paragraph{Standard use.}
The DPIR default (Gaussian, $\sigma=25$, $\lambda_{kz}=0.23$, \texttt{iter}=16) is data-starved
here and barely beats the no-prior baseline.

\paragraph{Optimal use.}
Raise $\lambda_{kz}$ into the band ($\approx12$), lengthen the schedule (\texttt{iter}${\approx}40$),
and pick the prior \emph{for the object}: there is \textbf{no universal best denoiser}. Measured,
\textbf{Gaussian} wins on the filament network, \textbf{TV~Bregman} on the continuous membrane under
noise, and the point-like case is split with DCT --- so the choice follows the structure and the

\paragraph{When it works, when it fails.}
Two intrinsic limits. \emph{Scale}: a denoiser is only a transferable prior if it is fed at the
scale it was built for --- the framework feeds $255\,f/\text{peak}$, which is what makes even DCT
usable on MA-TIRF (\emph{contradicting} the older claim that DCT always erases the image; that was
a scale artefact, removed by the $\div\text{peak}$ normalisation). \emph{Cost}: PnP runs a 3D
denoise on the full volume every schedule step, so on a large real stack it is memory- and
time-hungry (Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison}). Because it has no dual variable, PnP needs a high
$\lambda_{kz}$ (a strong prior driven into the null space) --- but the best denoiser is
object-dependent, not a fixed choice.
