\section{PPXA --- proximal splitting twin of Adam}
\label{sec:ppxa}

\paragraph{How it works.}
PPXA (Combettes--Pesquet 2008) minimises the same objective~\eqref{eq:objective} as Adam, but by
evaluating each term's proximal operator in parallel, averaging, and relaxing --- three terms
with equal weights: data, prior, positivity.
\begin{quote}\small
$p_1 \leftarrow \text{prox}_{\text{data}}(u_1) = (I+\gamma H^{\!\top}\!H)^{-1}(u_1+\gamma H^{\!\top} g)$;\quad
$p_2 \leftarrow \text{prox}_{\text{reg}}(u_2)$;\quad $p_3 \leftarrow \max(u_3,0)$;\quad
average and over-relax by \texttt{lambda\_relax}.
\end{quote}
Because it minimises the same convex $L$, with a prior that weighs it reaches the \emph{same
image} as Adam --- so ``do Adam and PPXA agree?'' tests the framework rather than being a
coincidence.

\paragraph{Parameters.}
Same solution axes as Adam (\texttt{reg}, $\lambda$, $\rho$). Its own knobs set speed only,
\emph{provided the proxes are exact}: the prox gain $\gamma$ must bracket the determined
directions, $\gamma \in [1/s_1^2,\,1/s_2^2] \approx [7\times10^{-4},\,3\times10^{-2}]$ (default
$0.01$); \texttt{lambda\_relax}$=1.5$ in $(0,2)$. Convergence knobs as Adam
(\texttt{EPS}$=10^{-8}$, \texttt{max\_iter}$=5000$, ridge $s_2^2$ start).

\paragraph{Standard and optimal use.}
Use it exactly as Adam --- match the prior to the object. The framework's precise claim is that
\emph{with $\gamma$ in its bracket and a prior that weighs, Adam and PPXA return the same image};
a disagreement is a diagnostic (the prior is too weak, or $\gamma$ is out of range). The campaign
runs one confirmation point (L1, $\lambda=0.2$, reference truth, noise 0.02 --- both solvers prox

\paragraph{When it works, when it fails.}
PPXA shares Adam's ceiling (only as good as the analytic prior) and adds two costs of its own:
it needs \emph{proximable} priors (Adam takes any differentiable one), and its proximal splitting
is markedly slower here --- in the earlier full grid its median runtime was several times Adam's,
with most configurations rejected or timed out. If $\gamma$ leaves its bracket, or the prior's
inner prox is inexact (TV/SHV), PPXA can stall \emph{above} the true minimum. It buys no accuracy
over Adam on this problem; Adam is the cheaper default.
