\section{ADMM --- sparsity by soft thresholding}
\label{sec:admm}

\paragraph{How it works.}
ADMM is the MAP estimator for a \emph{sparse}, non-negative object: its prior is hard-wired
($\ell_1$ + positivity), so it ignores $R,\lambda,r$. It alternates a data step, a soft
threshold and a dual update:
\begin{quote}
$t \leftarrow \kappa\,\max(g)/s_1$ \hfill\textit{(soft-threshold level)}\\
\textbf{repeat} for each iteration:\\
\hspace{1.2em}$u \leftarrow (H^{\!\top}\!H+\mu I)^{-1}(H^{\!\top} g + \mu(f-\eta))$ \hfill\textit{(data step: ridge fit pulled to $f-\eta$)}\\
\hspace{1.2em}$f \leftarrow \max(u+\eta-t,\,0)$ \hfill\textit{(soft-threshold at $t$, then $f\ge0$)}\\
\hspace{1.2em}$\eta \leftarrow \eta + \mu(u-f)$ \hfill\textit{(dual update)}
\end{quote}
\noindent where $\kappa$ (\texttt{threshold\_ratio}) sets the threshold $t$, $\eta$ is the dual
variable, and $\mu$ the penalty. The Laplacian prior $\exp(-\tau\lVert f\rVert_1)$ suits point-like
and filamentary objects, not a continuous membrane.

\paragraph{Key parameters.}
$\kappa$ sets the sparsity but its useful band cannot be predicted a priori (the data step spreads
the object across the null space, so a diffuse truth can be emptied by a modest $\kappa$); sweep it
log-spaced and raise it with the noise. $\mu$ carries three coupled roles at once (data-step ridge,
prior weight $\tau=\mu t$, dual step) and is capped: $\mu>1.618$ diverges. \texttt{iter}$=200$.

\paragraph{Limit.}
The wall is the prior. On a diffuse or continuous object $\ell_1$ is the wrong model, so there is
little-to-no useful $\kappa$ band --- $\kappa$ too large empties the image (the dominant failure),
$\kappa\approx0$ is noisy. Use ADMM for sparse objects at low noise; do not expect it to reconstruct
a membrane.
